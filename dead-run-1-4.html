Dead Run

An endless run-and-gun through a dead city. A Director watches how well you play and retunes the horde while you run.

The problem

Fixed difficulty makes a game too easy for skilled players and too hard for beginners — and a difficulty menu just asks players to guess which one they are.

The solution

Dead Run's Adaptive Director continuously reads accuracy, kills and damage taken, and retunes the challenge in real time. No menu, no guessing.

What the Director watches

Every 4 seconds it grades your play and moves the threat level up or down. There is no difficulty menu.

AccuracyShots that hit, out of shots firedSharp shooting raises the threat level. Spraying lowers it.
KillsZombies dropped in the windowEach kill adds pressure, up to four per window.
Damage takenZombie hits and pit fallsEach hit cuts the threat level hard, and health drops appear more often at low levels.

Innovation

Unlike traditional difficulty selection, Dead Run does not ask the player to choose Easy, Medium or Hard. The game observes the player and dynamically adjusts the challenge.

What changes as threat climbs

Spawn gaps shrink from 2.6s to 0.55s.

Runners appear from level 3, and armored brutes from level 6.

From level 5 zombies arrive in bursts of two or three, and the pits between platforms get wider.

Controls

AD or arrow keys to move, Space or W to jump.

Hold J or X to fire, P to pause, M to toggle music, R to restart after a death. You can also click the game to start or retry.

On a phone, use the on-screen buttons. Rotate sideways for full-screen play, and slide your thumb between ◄ and ► to turn without lifting.

Pick Easy, Medium or Hard on the start screen to set your opening threat level — the Adaptive Director keeps tuning it from there. The level never ends, and a looping score plays throughout. The sky shifts zone every 250 m.
